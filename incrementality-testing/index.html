Measurement · Incrementality testing

Testing that proves what your advertising caused.

Controlled experiments in real markets that separate the sales you paid for from the sales you would have made anyway.

The idea

Baseline sales versus caused sales.

Some of your sales would happen with no advertising at all. Incrementality is the part your advertising actually caused, above that baseline. It is the only number that tells you what your budget is really buying.

Platform dashboards claim credit for sales they merely witnessed. Incrementality testing measures what changes when the advertising itself changes.

How a lift test works

A controlled experiment in real markets.

We split comparable markets into two groups. Ads run in the test markets and hold back in the control markets. Everything else stays the same, so the difference in sales between the groups is the measured effect of the advertising.

The chart shows the shape of a result: both groups track together before the test starts, then the exposed markets pull away. The gap is the lift.

Reading the result

Lift, incremental ROAS, and confidence.

A finished test reports three things: the lift itself, the incremental return on ad spend it implies, and the statistical confidence behind both. If a result is not strong enough to act on, we say so and design the next test.

What it anchors

The test results calibrate everything else.

Lift results are the ground truth that keeps the rest of measurement honest. They validate the media mix model and correct attribution where platforms overclaim, so every number we report traces back to something that was actually proven.

We run tests routinely: channel on and off, budget levels, and pilots before a new channel earns a place in the plan.
